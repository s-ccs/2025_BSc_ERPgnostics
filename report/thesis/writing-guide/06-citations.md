# Citations and Bibliography

## Citation Style

- For theses (where space is not an issue): use **author name and year**
  format: [Smith 2008], [Murphy and Chaplin 1999].
- Use alphabetic labels (not numeric) where possible — greatly enhances
  readability.
- Hyperlink all references and citations.

## Citation Rules

1. Citations are annotations, NOT part of speech.
   - BAD: "[1] shows that CNNs work well."
   - GOOD: "Smith et al. [1] show that CNNs work well."

2. Multiple citations at one point use a single cite command:
   - GOOD: "...gives good results [3, 5]"
   - BAD: "...gives good results [3][5]"

3. Citations generally PRECEDE punctuation (unlike footnotes).

4. Citation labels must not appear at the beginning of a line.

5. Do NOT cite a paper you haven't carefully read and understood.
   Misrepresenting cited work will annoy reviewers.

## Related Work Citations

- The goal is to show you know the field.
- Don't make prior work look bad to justify yours.
- State prior work's achievements neutrally, then explain your extension.
- Normal assumption: prior work is good, you're building on it.

## Bibliography Completeness

- Every cited document needs: date, author, and enough info for the reader
  to find it.
- Don't abuse "technical report" category — use it only for documents in
  formal TR series with proper numbers and institutions.
- When citing web pages: add a timestamp ("Visited 1 May 2025").
- Don't abbreviate conference/journal names in theses — you have plenty of
  space.

## Bibliography Placement

- Standard rules say bibliography goes before appendix, but Heiser recommends
  putting it AFTER the appendix for easier access (since readers refer to
  the bibliography frequently).
